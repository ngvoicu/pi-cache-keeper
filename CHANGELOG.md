# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

## [0.7.0] - 2026-11-22

### Added
- **`/cache-keeper-stop`: stop keeping idle caches warm in every Pi window, until each one's next message** — Typed in any window, it ends warming for the idle period every window is in (for a window in a run, the period that run ends in) and for the ones that follow there: the extension sends no further refresh, and Pi's own idle warming is told to stop the next time it asks, also with `keepWarmMinutes: 0`. No setting changes, and it undoes itself: the answer to the next message you send a window (typed, over RPC, or sent in your name by another extension) starts an idle period that is warmed as usual. Nothing Pi or the extension sends by itself ends it. A stop is one more way a period's warming ends (`keep-warm ended (stopped by /cache-keeper-stop)` in the log, a `stopped:` line in `/cache-keeper`), and the footer says `warming stopped until your next message`. It is recorded in `cache-keeper-stop.json` beside the settings, which every window reads before it sends anything; a window obeys a record only if it is not the one it saw when your last message reached it, so no clock is compared and an old record stops nothing later. A refresh already on its way is not called back; a stop can't be taken back, since Pi's own warming, once stopped, starts again only with the next request; and compacting before the cache expires still runs where it is on, after which the window stays quiet: a summary starts no warming, the extension's or Pi's. Each other window says once that it was stopped. A stop holds through `/reload`, with two rare exceptions while Pi loads: Pi's own warming coming due then can send one refresh, and a message of yours that has not brought an answer yet lets the reload lift the stop before the next answer. Without a stop, `/reload` behaves as in 0.6.1.
- **Keep-warm says what it does** — Every refresh the extension sends and every issue it meets is now said in the transcript at once, whatever Pi's `showCacheMissNotices`. Every refresh keeps its own line: a saved entry in the session (`cache-keeper-refresh`, not sent to the model), drawn again after a reload, so an idle period shows as many lines as its budget allows refreshes (one cache miss divided by one refresh: 11 with a 1.25× write price and a cached read of a tenth of the input price, 48 at `claude-fable-5-2`'s prices; at most 10 without prices), each with its number, what it read, its cost and the period's sum (`Cache warmed (pi-cache-keeper, refresh 2): 99% of 600k from the cache, $0.18; $0.36 spent, a miss costs $2.07`); a miss, a failure, a refresh that reported no usage and one cancelled on its way say what they read and cost, in the warning colour where something went wrong. Everything else is a notice: a refresh held back or too late, warming ended and why, a period handed to Pi's own warming and why, a session left to Pi from its start (once per reason), Pi's own warming told to stop, a stop. Before, only a failure, a miss and a model left to Pi after two misses were said. A refresh that settles after a `/reload` keeps its line; one that settles while the reload is under way, or after `/new` replaced its session, may leave none (the log keeps its record). Each line is the log's record said aloud: nothing is logged that is not said. The refreshes are still not in Pi's session cost.

### Changed
- **An answer whose route a proxy does not name is a change of route** — `model-upstreams:answered` with `upstreams: []` was dropped, and the silence let the configured upstreams stand in, so a period could be warmed on a guessed route (a refresh writing the whole context again into another account's empty cache). It now means "this answer's route is unknown": for keeping warm it is a route of its own, unlike any other report, so a period that sees one is not refreshed again on that cache, and a period whose own answer had one is left to Pi's warming (`keep-warm: Pi's (the route is unknown)`). The footer's values are again those of what may serve the model.
- **The second miss in a row shows its own line** before the notice that the model is left to Pi; a failed refresh's and a miss's lines name what was read and spent.
- **A period handed to Pi because its request was let go says why in plain words** — `keep-warm: Pi's (you switched model or branch, or compacted)` in the footer, and in `/cache-keeper` and the log "you switched the model or the branch, or the conversation was compacted, so the extension no longer has the request to re-send", instead of "request let go".

### Fixed
- **Compacting before expiry is tried again when the first try was only postponed or the expiry moved** — Each countdown made one try, 20 s before expiry, whatever came of it. A try skipped because Pi was busy or a message was queued was not made again when the queue cleared, and when Pi's own cache warming then moved the expiry, the new one got no try either, so a large context could go cold uncompacted. Now a try postponed for a passing reason (Pi busy, a message queued, Pi's own refresh still in flight) is made again each second until the cache expires, and each expiry gets its own try. A lasting reason (compacting before expiry or Pi's auto-compaction off, Pi's settings unreadable, the context size unknown or under `minContextTokens`, the cached path unavailable) still spends the try for that expiry; it is checked again at a moved expiry, and each reason is written to the log once per countdown. A compaction that fails spends the try for the expiry it was made for, not for one Pi's refresh moved to while it ran. Every expiry tried is remembered until the next answer, so a retention switched to the long tier and back does not try the first expiry again. A `/cache-keeper-stop` does not change this: a stopped window still compacts before its cache expires, and the summary starts no warming.

## [0.6.0] - 2026-11-20

### Changed
- **Keep-warm: one clock of a period's cache, one owner of who warms it** — With keep-warm on, a period the extension took at its start has one clock (`cache-clock.ts`) that holds everything seen about its cache and gives one expiry: the period's answer, or the moment its latest refresh that reported a hit left, plus the shortest lifetime anything has shown since, which never rises again. It doesn't depend on who warms the period: a period whose warming ended on its budget, after a refresh that read 95% and wrote the rest into the 5-minute cache, is compacted 20 s before those five minutes end, while the cache is warm. The footer (its countdown and the lifetime it marks in use), `/cache-keeper`, the refresh and its last gate, compacting before expiry and the summary's last check all read it when they act. Who warms the period, and what Pi's own warming is told, is decided in one other place (`warm-period.ts`), which sees no lifetime. Pi's refreshes never move the expiry, also for a period whose request was let go. With keep-warm off from a period's start, its countdown, summaries and footer are as on 0.5.3, a proxy's report included (read at the countdown's next read, every 15 seconds). Switched off inside a period (in the settings menu, or saved from another window), the period's cache is read as with keep-warm off from then on: the countdown runs again from that reading, and compacting before expiry follows it.
- **Keep-warm: a route that changes leaves the cache's expiry unknown** — What a proxy reports served a request (`model-upstreams:answered`) is kept for each model on its own and compared whole: every field of the report, so a subscription login and an API key of one upstream are different routes; a report about another model never counts, and a model switch keeps the reports. A report names no request, so it is a period's own only where it came while the period's request was out (after it left, before its answer ended); where reports in that window differ, the period is Pi's (`the route changed`). A report of the model after the answer's end and before Pi goes idle may be another request's, a helper's on another account: where it differs from the answer's own, or where none came for the answer, the period is Pi's too. Where none came at all, the route's configured upstreams stand in, and a later first report that differs reads as a change (a proxy should report before the answer ends). The window gives timing, not a request: where the answer's own request was not reported, a lone report from Pi's own warming in that window is taken as the answer's route (at most one refresh into a cold cache in that period). A period declined at its start for its route is read as with keep-warm off, as on 0.5.3, so its countdown and compacting before expiry follow the plain clock. Where a proxy reports, for a period the extension took, that a request of the period's model was served by another route than the period's, also when the first route is reported again later, the period's expiry is unknown for the rest of it: the countdown reads cold, `/cache-keeper` says `counted as expired: the route changed`, and nothing is sent on that cache: no refresh, no summary before expiry (one already being prepared is cancelled at its last check, which reads the branch as it is then, a refresh of Pi's recorded during the preparation included), and a compaction for another reason uses Pi's own. The same holds where no lifetime can be read any more for a request that states none. This differs from 0.5.3 on purpose: where a gateway reports another route during a period, 0.5.3 could still send a summary before expiry that read the cache (Pi's refresh had just written the new route); now nothing is sent, and the next message after the cache expired pays to write the context again.
- **Keep-warm: an answer to a request Pi re-sent in another form is Pi's** — Where the provider rejects a request's strict tools (Anthropic, over its limits) and Pi re-sends it without them, the answer says so (`anthropic_strict_tools_fallback`), and the request the extension saw is the rejected one. Such a period is now left to Pi (`keep-warm: Pi's (request re-sent)`): the extension skips its keep-warm refresh for that period and leaves Pi's own warming free to decide. Before, the extension stopped Pi and re-sent the rejected form (a 400 again): on a route whose cache lasts an hour, a 600,000-token prompt's next message at 4,000 s wrote it all again ($3.60 instead of $0.18). Later requests of the session are built without strict tools from the start and are kept warm as before.
- **Keep-warm: after a hand-over, Pi's warming is stopped once nothing says the cache is alive** — A period handed over to Pi (its request let go, or evidence against its lifetime, see below) is told to stop only when nothing seen, Pi's own refreshes included (by when each was recorded), says the cache can still be alive; where no lifetime can be read, never. So Pi's refresh goes out while the cache lasts (0.5.3 kept Pi stopped, and the cache expired), and is stopped where it would only write a cache that is gone, at full price (on a 1,000,000-token prompt whose route fell from 5 minutes to 150 s, six such refreshes in half an hour). A period whose warming ended on its time limit or as `unable` keeps Pi's warming stopped until it is handed over, now also on evidence against its lifetime for one ended as `unable`; one ended on its budget, a miss, an unreported or a failed refresh keeps it stopped until the next request, as before. A period whose warming ended as `unable` is now handed over on evidence against its lifetime too, as one ended on its time limit is.
- **The footer after a hand-over** — Where Pi's warming keeps a period whose expiry has passed and whose route has not been seen to change, the footer and `/cache-keeper` count down to the moment nothing seen, Pi's refreshes included, says the cache written on that route is alive (`4m left by Pi's own warming …; nothing is sent on it`), instead of `cache cold`: an upper bound from what was seen, and nothing is sent on it. After a reported change of route both read cold, whatever Pi sent: nothing is known of the other route's cache. For a period the extension took, the tier marked in use is the one its request asked for and the lifetime the clock's, whoever warms it now: after a hand-over to a 5-minute write it reads `4m left`, `short 5m (in use)` and `⚠ sent short`, all the same five minutes. After a model switch away and back, a request that stated the hour reads `long 1h (in use)`.
- **Keep-warm: a period the extension can no longer refresh is Pi's** — When the extension let go of a period's request (a model switch, also away and back without a message; a move on the tree; a compaction), it still told Pi's warming to stop for the rest of the period, and then sent nothing itself: switching to another model and back left the cache to expire with neither warmer refreshing it (also on 0.5.3, for any period it kept warm). From that moment the period is now Pi's (`keep-warm: Pi's (request let go)`), and Pi's warming decides, stopped only once nothing seen says the cache is alive (above); also where its warming had already ended on its time limit. A period whose warming ended on its budget, a miss, a refresh that reported no usage or one that failed keeps Pi's warming stopped, as before.
- **Keep-warm: an answer's write is evidence of a lifetime, never the name of a tier** — Short and long are what a request asks for, how long a tier lasts is the route's, and Anthropic's 5-minute or 1-hour write in an answer's usage is evidence of a lifetime. A period's tier is now always its request's, and its lifetime the request's (the one a marker states, which has to be the data's; else the route's data for the tier asked). A write counts only where all of it went to one cache: a write partly in each confirms neither lifetime, and after one the model's periods are Pi's until an answer reports a write in one cache only (an answer that only read doesn't end that; such a write was read as an hour). Where the model's latest write on the branch lasts otherwise than the period's lifetime, the period is Pi's. A route whose long tier lasts 2 hours, asked for its short hour and answered with the 1-hour write, is now kept warm at the hour (it was Pi's: the write was read as the long tier); a route whose short tier lasts 5 minutes answered with the 1-hour write, or whose short tier lasts 30 minutes, is Pi's (it was Pi's before too, by the tier's name); a route whose data gives both tiers 5 minutes, answered with the 1-hour write, is now Pi's (it was kept warm at 5 minutes). Whether what is seen agrees with the period's lifetime is decided in one place (`lifetime-evidence.ts`), asked when the period starts and by the period's clock on every read (above). It reads what the period's answer wrote, the model's latest write up to it, what Pi's refreshes wrote after it, what the extension's own refreshes wrote (a refresh that read 95% and wrote the rest to the 5-minute cache was counted a hit and the hour kept; at 1 h 54 min it rewrote the whole context), and the route's lifetime for the request's tier read now. For a lifetime the request doesn't state, any write of another lifetime or into both caches, or the route now giving another, is against it; for one it states, after the start only what shows a shorter lifetime is. Against it, warming ends and the period is Pi's from then on: Pi's warming is told to stop only once nothing seen says the cache is alive (above; a period whose warming had ended on its time limit is handed over too; Pi's warming already told to stop in that period stays stopped). The period's clock counts the shortest lifetime read from the period's own last fact (above), a write into both caches as the shorter; it may read cold early, and compacting before expiry then sends nothing. An Anthropic Messages request without any cache marker (a gateway that caches on its own) has 5 minutes as its default, as a marker without `ttl` has: any other lifetime the route's data gives it needs the period's own answer to confirm it (it was kept warm by the data). Formats without such writes (OpenAI, Kimi) go by the data, need no confirmation and none of their reports is against, as before, except where the request carries a `cache_control` without `ttl`, which has its 5-minute default there too; Anthropic's own API is kept warm as before. `docs/keep-warm-coverage.md` has a section for two more routes, one whose short tier lasts an hour and one whose long tier lasts 30 minutes.
- **Keep-warm: a marker that states no lifetime takes the route's lifetime, where an answer confirms it** — Anthropic's `cache_control` without `ttl` asks for a tier and states no lifetime; with keep-warm on, its lifetime is now the one the route's data gives that tier, not Anthropic's documented 5 minutes. An answer's write is evidence of a lifetime (see the entry above). Where the route's lifetime for such a marker is not the 5 minutes (a proxy on a Claude subscription login, both tiers an hour), the period is kept warm only where its own answer reported a write, all of it in the cache of that lifetime; otherwise it is Pi's (`route's lifetime unconfirmed`), also where an earlier answer wrote that cache and the latest only read it (the route may have changed since). So that proxy with `"cacheRetention": "short"`, where the period's answer reports the 1-hour write, is now kept warm at the hour (refreshed at 57 minutes); before, it was Pi's ("the answer wrote the other tier"). An answer reporting the 5-minute write there (the proxy fell back to an API key) is kept warm at 5 minutes where the proxy said the key served it, and is Pi's otherwise, never kept at an hour. Anthropic's own API, and every route whose data agrees with the documented lifetimes, are kept warm as before; with keep-warm off nothing changes.

## [0.5.3] - 2026-11-20

### Added
- **The keep-warm goal in the footer** — With keep-warm on, the footer says so and for how long. While the extension keeps an idle period's cache warm it reads `keep-warm 2h until 16:40, $0.36 spent of $2.07`: the limit in force, the clock time it runs out (counted from Pi going idle), and what the period's refreshes have spent of the one cache miss they may cost (`3 of 10 refreshes` for a model without prices). A clock time, so it is not taken for the cache's `59m left`. Where nothing is being kept warm right now (a request runs, the cache is cold, no answer yet, after a model switch) it reads `keep-warm 2h`, the setting only. Ended, left to Pi and the other keep-warm texts read as before, and with keep-warm off nothing is shown. In a narrow terminal it shortens to `keep-warm until 16:40` and drops after the other lifetime, before the price; errors outlast it. Display only.

## [0.5.2] - 2026-11-20

On a route whose short tier lasts an hour (a subscription login behind a proxy, a gateway that gives the short tier an hour), a request for the short tier read `long 1h (in use) · short 1h · sent short`; it reads `short 1h (in use) · long 1h · sent short`. Display only.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-11-20

### Changed
- **The footer shows the values, what was sent, and what went wrong** — It used to mark what was known about the model (`· sub · window ✓ · cache short ✓ · long ✓ (in use) · price ✗`). It now says the values: `cache warm · 59m left · long 1h (in use) · short 5m · sent 1h · api $3/$15 per M`. The two cache lifetimes, the one in use first (the lifetime the countdown runs on); what the last request's cache markers asked for (`sent 1h`; `sent 5m (retention long)` when it asked for another tier than the retention in force; `⚠ sent 30m + 12h` when the countdown runs on another lifetime than the request asked for); and the price in use per million tokens at the current context size (`above 200k` where the price has tiers), or `sub, no per-token price` for a subscription login. What isn't known reads `unknown`; a guess stays marked `⚠` and a partial answer `?`. "sent" appears only for a request whose cache markers the extension reads (Anthropic Messages, OpenAI Chat Completions and Responses), and nothing is in use or sent for a model that has made no request in this session: after `/model`, the new model's lifetimes and price, with neither tier in use. The input window, which Pi's own footer shows, is no longer repeated. Every value is Pi's model as it is (its catalog, `models.json`, providers.dev's values written there), the lifetime the countdown runs on, or the request as sent; with model data off, and before a first fetch, the line shows Pi's own values.
- **Errors in the footer, right after the countdown** — `⚠ keep-warm ended (a refresh missed the cache)`, `(a refresh failed)`, `(a refresh reported no usage)` and `(the request can't be re-sent)` with the amounts, until the next message; `⚠ keep-warm: Pi's after 2 refreshes without a hit (/cache-keeper-warm)` for as long as it lasts, with a countdown or without (it showed only while a countdown ran); `⚠ answered by an API key`; `⚠ providers.dev: no data, fetch failed`; `⚠ providers.dev refresh failed: data 26h old` (it was `⚠ refresh failed: saved data`, at the end); and `⚠ countdown on another model's long tier` where, with keep-warm off, the countdown counts a tier another model's answer reported. Where the extension stepped back, muted: `keep-warm ended (budget reached): …` and `(2 h limit)`, `keep-warm: Pi's (thinking on)` for an idle period left to Pi's own warming, with the reason in a few words, and `keep-warm: from your next message`.
- **Fitted to the terminal's width** — Pi cuts the status line at the right edge. The line now shortens its wording first (`warm 59m · long 1h · short 5m · sent 1h · api $3/$15` at 60 columns), then drops the least important values (the providers.dev notes, what was sent when it agrees with the countdown, the other lifetime, the price, the lifetime in use), keeps errors to the last (down to a bare `⚠`), and is fitted again when the terminal is resized.
- **One status** — The countdown and the model's values are one status (`cache-keeper`), so the order on the line is the extension's.
- **The countdown in hours** past two hours: `11h 40m left`, not `700m left`; `/cache-keeper` too.
- **Keeping the cache warm: up to `keepWarmMinutes`, and it stops once refreshing has cost as much as one cache miss** — Refreshing every few minutes for the whole window could cost more than the one cache miss it avoids (a 150-second cache in a 30-minute window: about 14 refreshes, 1.4× the context's input price). Now the refreshes of an idle period (from one answer that read or wrote the cache to the next) stop once they have cost as much as one cache miss, worked out as Pi's own warming does (`evaluateCacheWarming`, computed by the extension with `calculateCost`, so Pi before 1.5.1 loads it too). A refresh goes out only if what was spent plus its reserve (its expected cost, or what the last refresh really cost when that was more) stays within one miss; what it reports replaces the reserve, and one that fails or is cancelled without a report stays counted. A refresh the provider does not serve from the cache costs a full write: it is counted, and warming ends. A model without prices gets at most 10 refreshes per period. A run that sent nothing starts no new period; a reload or restart does. Warming also ends at the time limit (said as soon as the cache lasts to its end), after a refresh that read under 90% from the cache, after one that failed or was cancelled before it reported a hit, after one that reported no usage, or when the request can no longer be re-sent; the footer (`· keep-warm ended (budget reached): $1.98 spent, a miss costs $2.07`), `/cache-keeper` and the log say which, with the amounts, and compacting before expiry takes over.
- **One warmer per idle period** — With keep-warm on, a period whose answer has a cache lifetime of at least a minute and a request the extension saw and can re-send is kept warm by the extension alone: each decision of Pi's own idle warming (`cache_warming_decision`) while Pi is idle is answered `stop`. Pi still warms during a run. A period the extension can't refresh itself (no lifetime, one under a minute, a last answer that used no cache, an answer that streamed past its cache's last safe refresh moment, Claude through pi-claude-bridge, Claude's budget-based thinking) is left to Pi's own warming, exactly as with keep-warm off, and the extension sends nothing; `/cache-keeper` says why. With keep-warm off nothing changes.
- **Only where a refresh is proven safe** — The extension keeps a period warm only for APIs whose Pi adapter is checked to build a one-token answer's output limit in the key the captured request carries it in (`anthropic-messages`, `openai-completions`, `openai-responses`), and only when the request carries that limit; Bedrock, Mistral, Gemini, Codex and every other API are left to Pi's warming. Every refresh passes one last gate, the only way out, read afresh in this order: not cancelled (tried again), keep-warm still on, the same period and not ended, the period's model, tier and lifetime unchanged, Pi idle with nothing queued (tried again), no earlier refresh of the period or the model still on its way (held), before the last safe moment, and an output limit within the API's proven one (1 token; 16 for OpenAI Responses). Anything else withholds it and ends warming. A refresh is built with the thinking level its payload shows. The tier of a request is recorded when it is sent (from its payload where it shows it, else the retention in force then; a tier the answer reports wins), and an owned period fixes the model, that tier and the model's lifetime for it; reading anything else later ends warming without another refresh, while a retention switched after the request changes nothing for it. Pi's own refreshes don't move the expiry of a period the extension keeps warm (see below).
- **By the request that was sent** — The cache tier of a period the extension keeps warm comes from the request as it went out, from every cache marker in it (Anthropic's `cache_control` anywhere, without `ttl` meaning 5 minutes; OpenAI's `prompt_cache_retention`, `prompt_cache_options` and `prompt_cache_breakpoint`; Kimi's 1-hour option), never from the retention setting; no marker is the default cache. Markers that ask for different lifetimes, an answer that reports writes in the other tier or both, and a lifetime only assumed for a model family off that provider's own API leave the period to Pi.
- **Two refreshes in a row without a hit, then Pi** — A refresh that reads under 90% of the prompt it reported from the cache is a miss, however it ended; one that settled successfully without a prompt in its reported usage did not report what it cost, and counts as a miss does; one cancelled or failed before it reported anything doesn't count. Either kind ends warming for that idle period; two in a row on a model, in any mix, leave that model to Pi's own warming, also a period already running, until `/cache-keeper-warm` or a restart of Pi (`/new`, `/resume`, `/fork` and `/reload` keep it). The user is warned once, with what the two were; the footer, `/cache-keeper` and the settings show it while it lasts. A wrong cache lifetime or unreported charges cost at most two refreshes without a hit, plus one for each refresh still open 10 minutes after it was sent; a route that alternates misses and hits is not cut off and pays a full write for each miss, at most one per idle period.
- **One refresh after another, each only after a hit** — Inside an idle period the extension's refreshes are waiting, out, or ended. While one is out nothing else is sent for the period, also after a run that sent nothing cancelled it, Pi's own refresh landed, or the 10-minute release passed; only its settlement moves the period on. A reported hit, however the refresh ended afterwards, makes it waiting again with the expiry counted from that refresh's send time; a miss, a success without a prompt in its usage (what it cost is unknown), or a failure or cancellation before it reported anything ends warming for the period. A refresh that has gone out is never cancelled for being slow nor sent again.
- **No refresh on top of one still on its way** — No refresh of a model leaves while an earlier one of that model, from any session of the Pi process, has not settled; it is held until the period's last safe moment. One still open 10 minutes after it was sent no longer holds the model back in other periods and sessions; its own period still waits for it. Once the second refresh in a row without a hit settles, a period already running is Pi's too: Pi's warming is no longer told to stop.
- **Prices in tiers count as prices** — A model is counted in refreshes only when every rate in every price tier is zero; otherwise the budget is in money at the rates for the request's size.
- **`/cache-keeper-warm`** — starts keeping the cache warm again for the models left to Pi after two refreshes without a hit, and clears every model's count; at the latest from the next message, without sending anything or turning keep-warm on.
- **Owned or left alone** — A period where one refresh would cost as much as the miss it avoids is left to Pi from its start (counted in refreshes only for a model with no price at all; one that charges only for output is counted in money), and a period left to Pi is read exactly as with keep-warm off (countdown, footer, compacting before expiry, log). A request with thinking on is left to Pi with a reason that names what it carries, for any provider. A cache marker of an unknown `type` or `mode` is not read.
- **Narrower where in doubt** — A request with more than one cache lifetime (GPT on OpenAI's Responses API with the default longer cache: the 12-hour shared prefix and the 30-minute conversation) or a stated lifetime that differs from the model data is left to Pi's warming. Keep-warm is read from the saved settings for ownership, Pi's warming decisions, the countdown and the last gate, so a switch-off in one window holds in all; unreadable settings send nothing and leave Pi's decision alone. A refresh limit must be a positive integer. The README lists which providers and requests the extension keeps warm and which are left to Pi.
- **Compacting before expiry checks the expiry again just before the summary leaves** — With keep-warm on or off: if the cache expired while the request was being prepared (a slow credential lookup, a proxy that just showed a shorter lifetime), nothing leaves and the compaction is cancelled, with the reason in the log. Through pi-claude-bridge the check is made when the request is handed to the bridge. Compacting when full and `/compact` are unchanged.
- **Keeping warm has no size threshold** — A refresh and a miss both scale with the context, so the budget holds at any size. `minContextTokens` keeps its name and now applies only to compacting before expiry.
- **A refresh held back is tried again** — When Pi is busy or a message is queued at the refresh moment, the refresh is tried again every second until the last safe moment (half its lead before expiry). A payload that would leave after that is withheld, and nothing is refreshed after expiry.
- **The expiry of a period the extension keeps warm comes from that period's own facts** — The answer that started it (including one that was cancelled or failed after reading or writing the cache), or the moment the period's latest refresh that reported a hit left, whichever is later, plus the period's lifetime. Pi's refresh entries, a slow preparation and a late settlement of an earlier period's refresh move nothing; the footer, `/cache-keeper`, the refresh timer, its last safe moment and compacting before expiry read this one value. A period whose answer streamed past that expiry's last safe moment before Pi went idle is left to Pi (`no time to refresh`). It never counts a longer lifetime than the one read now for the period's request: where a proxy shows a shorter one (in an answer or in Pi's own refresh), every reader counts with it from then on, so no summary is sent on its own after it has passed; where none can be read, the cache counts as expired. The period's facts hold only while the branch in front of you holds its answer, so after `/tree` to another branch the countdown is that branch's own, counted from its answer and tier as with keep-warm off; nothing of the period left behind carries over, also after keep-warm is switched off. With keep-warm off, and in a period left to Pi, the expiry is counted as before.
- **More time limits for keeping warm** — `/cache-keeper-settings` now offers 6 h, 12 h and 24 h besides 30 min to 8 h.
- **The log says why** — With keep-warm on, `expiry` and `warm` records carry the model, the cache lifetime and where it came from; `warm` records carry what the period has spent against its budget and, on the one record where warming ended, why (`budget`, `limit`, `missed`, `failed`, `unable`, `unknown`). Refreshes cancelled by a new request and the stop sent to Pi's warming are logged too. Older records read as before.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-16

First release of pi-cache-keeper. Needs Pi 0.87.0 or newer.

pi-cache-keeper is based on [pi-cached-compaction](https://github.com/nicobailon/pi-cached-compaction) by Nico Bailon (MIT), starting from its 0.2.0 release; his releases of pi-cached-compaction are kept below as he wrote them. Compaction from the warm cache, the compaction card, the cache countdown, compacting before the cache expires and the longer cache are his work. Added here since then: compacting when the context is full (`compactAtPercent`) or at a token cap (`compactAtTokens`), a summary sized to the model's window, a settings menu, a cache countdown that says what it means for cost, and keeping the cache warm while Pi is idle (`keepWarmMinutes`, off unless you set it). This is a separate project; it is not maintained by Nico Bailon.

### Changed
- **New name** — The package is `pi-cache-keeper`, installed with `pi install git:github.com/ngvoicu/pi-cache-keeper`. The commands are `/cache-keeper` and `/cache-keeper-settings` (were `/cached-compaction` and `/cached-compaction-settings`), the settings key is `cacheKeeper` (was `cachedCompaction`), and the log is `~/.pi/agent/cache-keeper.jsonl` (was `cached-compaction.jsonl`).
- **Settings carry over** — While `settings.json` has no `cacheKeeper` key, the `cachedCompaction` key is read as it is. The settings menu saves to `cacheKeeper`, starting from what it read, and leaves `cachedCompaction` alone. Compaction cards written by pi-cached-compaction still show in old sessions.
- **Labeled cache countdown** — The footer reads `cache warm · 59m left`, then `cache cold · next message full price`, instead of `⏱ 59m`, so it says what the countdown means for cost and is not mistaken for elapsed time or a countdown to compaction. During a request it reads `cache in use` instead of disappearing, so the footer no longer changes shape between turns.

### Added
- **Compact when the context is full** — After a turn, once the context fills `compactAtPercent` of the model's window (90% by default), the session compacts from the warm cache while a summary still fits beside the conversation. Before, a 1M-token session reached Pi's own threshold with about 16k tokens left, the cached summary hit its output limit, and Pi's compaction ran at full price ($4.20 instead of about $0.45 on a 984k-token Opus session).
- **Token cap for compacting when full** — `compactAtTokens` (off by default) compacts once the context holds that many tokens, on any model, at the cap or `compactAtPercent`, whichever comes first. A percentage is a different size on every window; a cap keeps the context, and so the price of each request's cache read, under one size everywhere.
- **Keep the cache warm when idle** — `keepWarmMinutes` (off by default) keeps the prompt cache warm for that long after Pi goes idle: shortly before it expires, the last request is re-sent exactly as sent with a one-token answer, as Pi's own warming does. Pi's warming stops 30 minutes after the last request and never refreshes a 1-hour cache while idle. A refresh that fails or misses the cache stops warming until the next message. When the window ends, compacting before expiry takes over.
- **`/cache-keeper-settings`** (also `/cache-keeper settings`) — A menu for every setting (compact when full and at what token cap, compact before expiry and from what size, keep the cache warm, cache retention). Changes are saved to `cacheKeeper` in `~/.pi/agent/settings.json` and apply right away. `/cache-keeper` shows the "when full" mark for the current model and the keep-warm state.

### Fixed
- **Summary length** — The cached summary may use the model's output limit, reduced so the conversation and the summary fit the window, like Pi's own compaction. It was capped at 80% of `compaction.reserveTokens` (about 13k tokens), too short for large sessions. With less than 16k tokens of room, the cached path is skipped instead of attempted.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-22

First release. Needs Pi 0.87.0 or newer.

### Highlights
- Compaction reads the prompt cache your session already built. About 99% of the summary request comes from the cache instead of being billed at full price; on a 667k-token Opus session that was $0.19 instead of about $1.40.
- The summary is written from the full tool output, not the first 2,000 characters of each result.

- Every compaction shows a card comparing its cost with what Pi's own compaction would have cost.

### Added
- **Cached compaction** — The summary request repeats your conversation as it is (the same system prompt, tools and messages) and asks for the summary as the next turn. The result has Pi's format: older messages summarized, recent ones kept word for word, and the read and modified file lists carried forward. If the request can't be shown to match your last one byte for byte, or the model tries to call a tool, Pi's own compaction runs.
- **Compaction card** — After each compaction, a card under the summary shows how much came from the cache, the cost and the time, next to Pi's estimated cost for the same messages. Expand it with ctrl+o to see what the decision rested on. Every decision is also logged to `~/.pi/agent/cached-compaction.jsonl`.
- **Cache countdown and compacting before expiry** — The footer counts down the cache's remaining life (`⏱ 59m … ⏱ 0:25`, then `cache cold`). When Pi is idle with at least 50k tokens of context, the extension compacts 20 seconds before the cache expires. Turn it off with `compactBeforeExpiry: false`, or raise `minContextTokens`, under `cachedCompaction` in `~/.pi/agent/settings.json`.
- **Longer cache in interactive sessions** — Asks for the 1-hour cache on Anthropic, and a 12-hour cache for the system prompt and tools on GPT-5.6 and later, so ordinary breaks don't expire it. Set `cacheRetention: "short"` to keep Pi's default.
- **`/cached-compaction`** — Shows the cache right now, whether the next compaction would read it, and this session's compactions. `/cached-compaction cache-flag` turns on OpenAI's cache options for GPT models on opencode-go, which Pi doesn't send by default.
- **Providers** — Works with Anthropic, Claude on opencode-go, Claude through pi-claude-bridge, GPT-5.6 and later on `openai` and opencode-go, other Responses and Chat Completions APIs, and `openai-codex`. Google and other providers keep Pi's own compaction.
